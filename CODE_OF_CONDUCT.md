# FlashGate MCP Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

FlashGate MCP is committed to a welcoming, safe, and professional community.
Everyone who participates in project spaces is expected to help create an
environment free from harassment and discrimination, regardless of background,
identity, experience, ability, or level of contribution.

## Expected behavior

Community participants must:

- communicate respectfully and assume good faith while still addressing
  technical and security risks directly;
- give and receive constructive feedback about the work rather than the person;
- respect boundaries, privacy, confidential reports, and requests to stop
  unwelcome conduct;
- make project discussions accessible and focused, and welcome questions from
  people with different levels of experience;
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest and prioritize community safety; and
- accept responsibility, apologize when appropriate, and correct harmful
  behavior.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is not accepted:

- harassment, discrimination, threats, intimidation, stalking, or sustained
  disruption;
- insulting or demeaning comments, personal attacks, trolling, or unwanted
  sexual attention;
- publishing another person's private information without explicit permission;
- deliberately exposing credentials, vulnerabilities, or other sensitive
  security information through public project channels;
- retaliation against someone who reports a concern or assists an enforcement
  review; or
- other conduct that a reasonable person would consider inappropriate in a
  professional community.

## Scope

This code applies in repository discussions, issues, pull requests, reviews,
other official project spaces, and when someone represents FlashGate MCP in a
public or private setting. It also applies to interactions that materially
affect a person's ability to participate safely in the project.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to the project maintainers through an
available private GitHub contact channel. If no private channel is available,
open a minimal issue asking a maintainer to establish private contact; do not
include sensitive details in that issue. Do not include credentials,
vulnerability details, or other security-sensitive information in a conduct
report's public metadata.

Include the relevant dates, project spaces, behavior, and any supporting links
or records that can be shared safely. A reporter may identify desired safety
measures, but maintainers determine the response after reviewing the available
information. Knowingly false reports or retaliation are themselves violations;
an unsubstantiated good-faith report is not.

## Enforcement

Maintainers responsible for enforcement will:

1. acknowledge a report when a secure reply channel is available;
2. limit report details to the people needed for a fair review;
3. identify conflicts of interest and recuse affected decision-makers;
4. give the reported person a reasonable opportunity to respond when doing so
   does not create an immediate safety risk;
5. document the decision and proportionate action privately; and
6. communicate the outcome to the reporter and affected participant as far as
   privacy and safety permit.

Maintainers may take immediate temporary action to protect participants or
project infrastructure while a report is reviewed. Proportionate outcomes may
include a private clarification, warning, required corrective action, temporary
participation restriction, removal of content or privileges, or a permanent ban
from project spaces. Repeated behavior, retaliation, credible threats, and
deliberate disclosure of private or security-sensitive information warrant a
stronger response.

Enforcement decisions may be reconsidered when material new information becomes
available. A request for reconsideration must identify the decision, the new
information, and any conflict or process error; disagreement alone does not
require a new review.

## Project boundary

This code governs participation in FlashGate MCP spaces. It does not replace
platform terms, applicable law, employer policies, or the project's technical
security and contribution requirements. Maintainers may also report conduct to
the relevant hosting platform or appropriate authorities when necessary to
protect people or comply with legal obligations.
